Text reclaims VISUAL dimension without becoming purely image. You SEE structure while READING content. ### THE SHIFT: **Traditional Writing**: - Linear (one word after another) - Temporal (experienced in time) - Sequential (paragraph → paragraph) - Hierarchical (outline-based) **Topographic Writing**: - Spatial (arranged in space) - Visual (structure is visible) - Networked (multiple paths) - Relational (connections matter as much as content) "Although the computer is not necessary for topographical writing," Bolter notes, "it is only in the computer that the mode becomes a natural and, therefore, also a conventional, way to write" (p. 618). ### VISUAL COHERENCE (Diane Balestri, p. 618): Hypertext requires "training ... in the new habits of thought necessary to perceive coherence in patterns and links, and to generate coherent patterns and links of their own." Traditional literacy: Follow linear argument Topographic literacy: Perceive spatial structure, navigate networks, recognize patterns #### MY NOTE (p. 618): "We are working many years after these texts have been theorized, reported and published. Do we think these skills are now more innate as evidenced by how far we have come along with novel technologies and development of GUIs?" Question: Are we MORE topographically literate now (post-Web, post-mobile)? ### COUNTER-NOTE (p. 619): "Speaking of the development of GUI, how has the iOS-ification of seemingly all user-centric technology perhaps taken us aback from these literacies with the trade-off being convenience?" Alternative: Have simplified interfaces made us LESS able to see/build structure? We can swipe, but can we map? ### STRUCTURE SEARCHES (Frank Halasz, p. 619): Beyond content search (find words), we need structure searches (find patterns). Example query: "Find all cycles in the link structure" Purpose: "Could be used, for example, to find circular arguments" In topographic writing, VISUAL RIDDLE easier than VERBAL RIDDLE: - Linear text: Circular argument hidden in sequence - Spatial map: Circular structure immediately visible Applications: - Code: Finding circular dependencies - Arguments: Revealing logical loops - Networks: Identifying feedback cycles - Narratives: Discovering recursive structures #### MY NOTE (p. 616): "I am trying to implement transparency in this demonstration today by first including the overall organizational view of this Twine. Likewise, y'all are welcome to look at the twee files for your own adaptation and consideration." Making structure visible = topographic principle. You can SEE the map, not just navigate blindly. ### MISSING LINKS & NEW FORMS (p. 619): "It is not too far-fetched, I think, to suggest that the ideas of Douglas, Balestri, Bolter, and Halasz open the possibility that hypertext learning tools may result in the discovery of what might, in a bad pun, be termed missing links, i.e., novel structures of thought and new rhetorical forms." These new forms "promise (or, if you will, threaten) to rival, or even supplant, the structures we have come to believe are more god-given than Gutenbergian." The essay, the book, the argument—NOT natural forms. They're artifacts of PRINT TECHNOLOGY. Topographic writing enables forms we haven't imagined yet. #### MY NOTE (p. 619): "Do you think we have realized the full-potential of these views? Once you THINK topographically, it feels natural. But it's invisible to those who don't. #### MY NOTE (p. 619): "My experience using Markdown, frankly.
