# You're Already Rhetorical

## Williams: The Big Myth
- "Popular culture is killing writing"
	- Been around since 1887
	- Still false today
- *Students write MORE than ever before*
	- Social media, text messages -- neither of which are exclusive to academics.
## What You Already Know
- You understand rhetoric through practice:
	- Genre conventions (rom-com vs. horror)
	- Audience expectations (TikTok vs. LinkedIn)
	- Style choices (text to friend vs. email to me)
## The Transfer Challenge
- Your rhetorical knowledge CAN transfer
	- Not automatic
	- Requires practice with new genres
- *Essentially it's the same way you've learned pop culture*

## Rhetoric Isn't Dirty
- Ancient tools for civic participation:
	- **Ethos**: credibility, trust
	- **Pathos**: emotion, connection
	- **Logos**: logic, reasoning
## Kairos: Timing Matters
- Right message + right moment = impact
	- "TTYL" to your friend? Fine.
	- "TTYL" to your professor? Different story.
- *Context shapes everything*
## Rhetorical Inventory
- **Format**: Think-Pair-Share
- **Groups**: 5 people per group
- **Materials**: Your own pen and paper
## Instructions
- **5 minutes**: Write individual responses to prompt
- **10 minutes**: Share with your group of 5
- **5 minutes**: Groups share insights with class
*Take out paper and pen now*
## Prompt 1: Genre Recognition
**Write for 5 minutes:**
- Describe a time this week when you switched between different types of communication (texting, email, social media, formal writing, conversation, etc.).
- What was the situation? What genre did you use? How did you KNOW which genre to use?
## Rhetoric in Your Life
- **Write (5 min):** Recall a text/email you adapted for a specific person, OR a genre you know well (group chat, cover letter, IG caption), OR a time you persuaded someone.
	- What did you do? Why?
## Rhetoric in Your Life
- **Discuss (15 min):** Share your examples.
	- What patterns emerge? Where did you learn these moves?
	- Connect to Williams' "practice" and the rhetorical triangle.
	- Choose one example to share with class.
## Whole Class Debrief
- Each group: Share ONE interesting example or insight from any of the three prompts.
- *What are we learning about ourselves as communicators?*
## You already do this work
- You analyze audience
- You adapt to genre conventions
- You make strategic rhetorical choices
- *This course helps you do it consciously in new contexts*
