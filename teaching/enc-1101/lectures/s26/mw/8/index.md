# Multimodality and Visual Rhetoric
## Recap: Li on Literacy Narratives

- Li showed you three students reflecting on language and identity

- Jlenar's story about "water" at Starbucks—one mispronounced letter revealing cultural assumptions and the need for empathy

- Hala rediscovering Arabic literature while abroad—realizing her cultural heritage shapes how she sees the world

*These stories aren't just personal—they analyze how literacy and identity intertwine*

## Recap: Anzaldúa's Wild Tongue

- Anzaldúa refuses to be ashamed of Chicano Spanish

- "Ethnic identity is twin skin to linguistic identity—I am my language"

- Code-switching isn't confusion—it's navigating multiple worlds, multiple identities

*Her argument: You can't separate who you are from how you speak*

## Why These Readings Matter for Your MLN

- Both authors model what you're doing: analyzing a literacy experience to understand your identity

- Li gives you the narrative structure (story + reflection)

- Anzaldúa shows you how to make claims about language and power

*Your MLN should do both—tell your story AND analyze what it means*

## Carroll: You Already Do This Work

- You analyze rhetoric every day without thinking about it

- Your professor's backpack vs. briefcase? That's rhetorical analysis.

- How someone dresses, talks, presents themselves—you're constantly reading these signals

*Carroll just wants you to do it consciously and systematically*

Carroll breaks down context into three parts:

- **Exigence**: What problem is being addressed?

- **Audience**: Who needs to hear this? Who can act?

- **Constraints**: What limits how the message gets delivered?

## Logos, Pathos, Ethos

How does rhetoric actually persuade you?

- **Logos**: Appeals to logic, reason, facts

- **Pathos**: Appeals to emotion, values, identity

- **Ethos**: Appeals to credibility, trust, authority

## Think-Pair-Share: "Reading the Room"

- Think about a recent situation where you had to "read the room" and adapt how you communicated

- Could be a text message, an email, how you dressed, what you said in a meeting

- Use Carroll's framework: What was the exigence? Who was your audience? What were your constraints?
