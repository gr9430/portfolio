THE TEXTS THAT GROUND THIS SEMESTER
Three lenses for
reading the world.
BARTHES, ROLAND.
"Rhetoric of the Image"
Image-Music-Text, pp. 32–51
How do images communicate meaning? Barthes argues images carry layers of denotation, connotation, and ideology — and that reading them is as rhetorical as reading words.
BENJAMIN, WALTER.
"The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction"
Illuminations, pp. 1–26
What happens to meaning when art can be infinitely copied? Benjamin asks who controls the image — and what "authenticity" even means once reproduction is possible.
McLUHAN, MARSHALL
"The Medium is the Message"
Understanding Media, pp. 1–18
It's not just what you say — it's the form you say it in. McLuhan argues the medium itself shapes how meaning is made and received.
Research is not
Googling until you find something that agrees with you
Summarizing what others have said
Proving a point you already believe
Research is
Asking a question you genuinely don't know the answer to
Listening to what the literature actually says
Placing your own interpretation in conversation with others

A counter-narrative
“Popular Culture
is Killing Writing”
Bronwyn T. Williams
“We rarely trust the imposition of a new magic on our lives, and we rarely fail to work up nostalgia for the older magic it replaces.”
— Mitchell Stephens, qtd. in Williams
THE CRISIS NARRATIVE — OLD AS DIRT
1887: Harvard's Adams Sherman Hill warned that newspapers and “second-rate novels” were destroying student writing. Same complaint, every generation — just swap the medium: comics, television, video games, social media, AI.
WHAT THE RESEARCH ACTUALLY SHOWS
Today's students write more than any prior generation — longer papers, fewer errors (Lunsford, Stanford). Pop culture engagement builds exactly the rhetorical skills we need: audience, genre, and style.
CONNECTING BACK
Barthes gives us tools to read images rhetorically. Benjamin asks who controls reproduction. McLuhan reminds us the medium shapes the message. Together — these are exactly the lenses you need to take pop culture seriously.
So why is this the focus of your project?
