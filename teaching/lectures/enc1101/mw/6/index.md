## The Bridge: Translation → Code-Switching

- **De Paor showed us**: Translating Irish → English changes meaning, can displace the original

- **Harrison showed us**: "Translating" Yorkshire → RP meant losing his voice to access prestige

- **Today's reading asks**: What happens when you live BETWEEN languages/codes constantly?

	- Not just switching once, but navigating multiple codes daily
	- Not just translation—code-switching, code-meshing, translanguaging

## Today's Focus: Spanglish

- **Suarez**: Spanglish is legitimate (not "broken" English or "bad" Spanish)

_Code-switching is strategic linguistic work_

## Suarez: Spanglish is Real

- Not "broken English" or "bad Spanish"

- It's a legitimate linguistic practice with its own rules

- Code-switching follows patterns—it's strategic, not confused

*Your bilingualism or bidialectalism is a strength*

## Suarez: Examples of Code-Switching

Suarez gives specific examples:

- "Parquin" for parking (shorter than "estacionamiento")

- "Linkear" for click (adapted for computers)

- Borrowing replaces longer Spanish words with shorter English ones

*This isn't laziness—it's efficiency and adaptation*

### Critical Language Awareness

- What Suarez wants us to understand:

	- Language mixing is purposeful
	
	- Speakers make conscious choices about when to switch
	
	- Different contexts require different codes
	
	- There's no "pure" language—all languages borrow and mix
